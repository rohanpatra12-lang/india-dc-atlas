v362026-10-28Treated water break-even in 12.6. A new calculator asks whether treated sewage is cheaper than BWSSB drinking water for a campus, given its size, load, water use, the pipe length to the treatment plant, the treatment grade and how the pipe is financed. It plots the yearly saving or extra cost against distance and gives the break-even pipe length, with and without Karnataka's ₹8 lakh a year reimbursement. A callout checks a widely shared costing for a 50 MW Bengaluru campus: its arithmetic holds, but it uses ₹110/kL drinking water (top-slab water charge is ₹99), an unsourced ₹7.5/kL treatment cost that cannot reach cooling-tower grade without reverse osmosis, and interest-free pipe amortisation; it also wrongly says a 50/50 blend halves the result. Corrected, its 6–7 km break-even falls to about 4–5 km, or about 2–3 km with reverse osmosis. On cooling towers the same pipe pays back over about six times the distance.
v352026-10-28Training time in 14.6. The design check now has a training campaign setting: how many months a year the training hall is busy, and how much power it draws while idle between runs (about 20% of rated). Runs per year now count only busy months, while energy, water and carbon include the idle months. A new example design, "300 MW remote training campus", sets up a training-only site in an inland renewable zone in Andhra Pradesh: 100% training, N redundancy, B300 GPUs, warm-water liquid cooling with dry coolers, 85% renewable power and cheap land. A new city option, "Remote renewable zone", carries its climate, tariff and grid water. In 14.6, settings for a zone with no capacity are now greyed out (a training-only design shows its inference and general-purpose settings as not used), and the Visakhapatnam example is renamed "200 MW AI hub (75% training)" because it deliberately mixes training, inference and general-purpose halls.
v342026-10-28Easier to read. Every abbreviation and technical term is defined where it is first used in each section: about 270 short forms (PUE, CGWB, W40, NVL72, FY25…) and 40 terms (blowdown, wet-bulb, open access, colocation…) are underlined with dots; hover, tap or tab to one for its meaning. The Reference page has a searchable list of all of them. Sixty-eight tables and fact panels that had no explanation now have a "How to read this" guide saying what the table shows, how to read it, what it tells you and what to be careful about, so every chart, table and calculator in the atlas now carries an interpretation.
v332026-10-28Open source. The atlas is now built from editable source files (src/: one file per chapter, per script module and for the standards register) with a one-command build (python3 tools/build.py) that reproduces the published pages exactly, a browser check of every page, and open data downloads of the standards register and chip records (data/standards.csv, data/chips.csv). Code is MIT-licensed; text, data and research are CC BY 4.0. Report 14 is identified as the least-water power model (16.2).
v322026-10-27AI accelerators. The standards register (2026.10.6) gains one sourced record for each of nine chips: H100, H200, B200, B300, MI300X, MI325X, MI355X, Ironwood and Gaudi 3, with specifications, server power per GPU, MLPerf Inference Server throughput per GPU and rental prices. Chapter 11 reads them: 11.1 shows the register table and an MLPerf results table; 11.2 adds tokens per watt and per rupee, and measured training runs (Llama 3.1 405B energy corrected to 23.3 GWh with the building; ~30 GWh was all three sizes); 11.4 adds India's named deployments converted to megawatts; new 11.6 sizes a cluster for inference, training and the memory wall. The design check (14.6) adds B300 and MI355X and scales training-run energy by each GPU's speed and power; the data centre model (14.2) and the GPU-hour calculator (15.2) take throughput and power from the register. IndiaAI prices now label the subsidised user rate and the lowest bid separately.
v312026-10-27Explanations and consolidation. Every Data centre 101 calculation now says why its equations and values were chosen; three interactive panels that had no reading guide have one; the Reference page lists what each of Reports 1–15 is and where it is used, and defines the terms most easily mixed up (grid water at the plant and the meter, withdrawal and consumption, location and contract carbon, average and marginal factors, IT and all-in prompt energy, energy charge and all-in tariff, utilisation, occupancy and load). Chapter eyebrows link to the report list. Operator disclosure is gathered in 9.4 and per-prompt figures in 9.2, with one-line pointers where panels moved; the empty 11.5 is removed (11.6 becomes 11.5); Chapter 16 opens with a map of every cooling tool by the question it answers. Ch 9 stress weights are labelled as atlas judgements with their basis; the Chapter 10 hall comparison uses the register loads (65% and 78%).
v302026-10-27Audit fixes. Shared defaults: the forecast engine starts from the register grid factor (0.675) and uses register tower water (1.8 L/kWh); the Chapter 13 power-and-water panel, the Chapter 18 forecast conversions and the growth explorer now use the engine's 2030 fleet (4.66 GW, 59.5% load, PUE 1.43, 40% AI, 43% clean, 0.607 t/MWh); AI halls 78% and colocation 65% load everywhere; the design check drops import duty and insurance by default and calibrates serving efficiency to measured energy per prompt; 14.2 adds IT support and maintenance (2% of IT capital); Chapter 12 uses the ₹8.0/kWh all-in tariff. v292026-10-27Section 14.6, Design check, rebuilt as one native tool: the earlier embedded designer merged with a reader-supplied hybrid campus and grid-interactive tool, so 14.8 is no longer needed. Training, inference and general-purpose zones each have their own utilisation, rack density and cooling system (with its plant cost from the 14.2 tables); outputs add queries per day, training runs, cost per GPU-hour, battery and load-shifting economics, infrastructure (design-day peak, grid connection, generators, cooling plant, network, water plant, land), seven checks with the change that flips the verdict, a flip-the-cooling table, sensitivity and pinned comparisons, with a formula drawer behind every panel. 14.7 now points to it. Corrections to the supplied tool: GPU power 1.4–2.0 kW all-in, not 12.5 kW; battery price arbitrage was missing a ×1,000 and now counts round-trip losses; training-deferral savings were about 10× too high and are now limited by spare capacity; energy per query divided by tokens twice; demand-charge savings are limited to the shaveable peak of a flat load; GPUs priced at ₹266 crore/MW plus duty at ₹90 per US$; cooling towers at 1.8–2.4 L/kWh by climate; Bengaluru supply 1,935 MLD; unsourced global and reimbursement figures removed.
