    What reading across the chapters recommends

    • Quote three numbers, never PUE alone. Every site should report PUE at a stated IT load, on-site WUE (both withdrawal and consumption), and the grid water behind its electricity. Chapters 8, 9 and 16 show that the grid water is usually the largest of the three.
    • Use state factors, not national averages. Power is pooled nationally, so the atlas default is the India grid's 2.9 L/kWh; as a sensitivity, the same 50 MW site on its own state's generation mix carries about 1.5 L/kWh of grid water in Karnataka and 3.3 in Uttar Pradesh (register gwmix), a difference larger than most cooling choices make.
    • State how the load was derived. A conventional server that is busy 30% of the time still draws about 55% of its rated power, so energy and water per unit of work fall sharply when work is consolidated onto fewer, busier machines.
    • Count clean power hour by hour. Annual renewable certificates make a site look water-free on paper while it runs on coal power at night. Chapters 14 and 16 count physical hourly supply.
    • Prefer designs that do not hinge on unmeasured numbers. Reservoir hydro's water (0–20 L/kWh) is not measured anywhere in India; a solar, wind and storage mix gives the same answer under every assumption.
    • A gap for policy to fill. India publishes an official grid carbon factor but no official grid water factor. CEA or BEE publishing state-wise water intensity each year, alongside the CO₂ baseline, would let every operator and state report the same way this atlas now does.
