14.4Model · Money

Capex, the value it earns, and how long it lasts

A data centre is three assets with three lifetimes: a building that lasts 25–30 years, cooling and electrical plant that needs a refit around year 15, and IT hardware that is replaced every 4–6 years. Where the comparison numbers come from

    Data centre prices: wholesale colocation in India ~$78–100 per kW a month excluding power (~₹6,900–8,800); FY26 operator revenue ₹9.4–9.7 cr per MW including resold power (Nxtra, Sify). GPU-hour defaults are contracted rates below on-demand list prices (India H100 on-demand ₹218–400, 12-month reserved ~₹219, IndiaAI subsidised ₹67–92). H100 hyperscaler rents fell ~33% in a year to late 2025; neocloud rents held. Token prices: market median ~$0.55 input / $1.94 output per million tokens (2026), open 70B models ~$0.5–0.9. Hyperscalers depreciate servers over 6 years; neoclouds use 4–5. estimate

    • How the cash flow is built. Building and plant are paid over two construction years, and IT hardware at the end of the second. Revenue starts in year 1. In colocation, occupancy ramps 50% → 75% → target. Tenants pay rent plus electricity for their IT load multiplied by a contracted PUE, so a site that runs below the contracted PUE keeps the difference, and one that runs above it loses money. In GPU cloud and AI service, the operator owns and replaces the IT every refresh cycle. Each new generation starts at your price, and prices then fall each year until the next refresh.
    • Replacements and resale. Cooling is replaced at the end of its own life: room-side equipment (air handlers, CDUs, tanks) every 12–15 years and heat rejection (chillers, towers, dry coolers) every 15–20 years, at the cost shown in 14.3 for your technology. UPS, batteries and switchgear are refitted in year 15 at 20% of the building-and-electrical cost. At the end, the building keeps 10% of its value, and cooling and IT keep a straight-line share of their unused life.
    • Opex. Electricity and demand charges, water, building and electrical maintenance (1.5% of its capex), cooling maintenance (3–5% of cooling capex by technology) with tower water treatment and immersion-fluid top-up, staff, plus 10% of revenue (5% in colocation) for network, software and sales.
    • Not included. Land, taxes, debt, inflation, and the possibility that a new GPU generation earns more than the one it replaces.

    14.5Model · Limits

    What the model does and does not capture

    Scope and limits

    • Climate: monthly mean dry- and wet-bulb with a cosine daily swing (peak 15:00). Real years have heatwaves and humid spells; the design hour uses ASHRAE 0.4% conditions to size plant.
    • Equipment: generic efficiencies, not a specific vendor. Chiller COP = 0.40–0.45 × Carnot, limited to 2–10. Real plants vary ±10–15%.
    • Water: on-site water counts evaporation, blowdown, spray and humidification; power-station water uses the India grid, 2.9 L/kWh at the meter (3.8 at the margin).
    • AI output: tokens per second per GPU from MLPerf-style results scaled by model size, precision and a production batching factor; real services vary several-fold with context length and latency targets.
    • Costs and value: tariffs and capex are indicative 2026 figures; land, taxes and financing structure are not included. Revenue prices in 14.4 are your inputs, and comparison projects use sector averages, not a specific plant.
    • Not modelled: part-load chiller curves, humidity control in detail, fouling, and the transient power swings of training jobs.

    Design verdictSeven checks, each pass, caution or fail. The binding constraint is the worst check, and the line under the verdict gives the smallest single change that would flip it.

    How this is calculated · Design verdict
    1. Cooling: each zone's rack density ≤ its cooling system's rack ceiling (caution within 15%); dry-cooled liquid loops also need design ambient ≤ supply water − 6 °C
    2. Power: design-day facility peak ≤ 80% of the grid connection passes, 80–100% caution, above fails
    3. Water: peak-day potable draw against the allocation (80% / 100%), or, with no allocation, against a screening limit for the city's water stress; on-site WUE above 1.5 L/kWh in a high-stress city fails
    4. Regulatory (India): PUE above 1.35 (asked in MeitY AI-cloud tenders) or evaporative cooling on 50%+ potable water in a high-stress city → caution
    5. Carbon: delivered intensity = grid factor × (1 − renewable share); ≤ 300 g/kWh pass, ≤ 400 caution, above fails
    6. Grid-interactive: battery payback ≤ 6 years pass, ≤ 10 caution, otherwise fail (no battery → pass)
    7. Site: land needed against land available (80% / 100%), when entered
    8. Flip: the binding check's main driver is changed in 1% steps (up to 60%, or 100% for renewables) until the overall verdict changes
    Assumptions
    • The 300 and 400 g/kWh lines, the 1.5 L/kWh water line and the stress screening limits (high 500/2,000; medium 1,000/4,000 kL/day of potable water on a peak day) are screening thresholds for comparing designs, not regulations.
    • Water stress labels by city are indicative, not a basin study.

    Infrastructure: power chain, cooling plant, network, water plant and landWhat has to be built to carry the design: grid connection, transformers, generators, cooling plant, the GPU network, the water treatment train and the land.

    How this is calculated · Infrastructure: power chain, cooling plant, network, water plant and land
    1. Zone design-day peak (MW) = zone IT MW × [1 + (zone PUE − 1 − 0.04) × (1 + k × (design dry-bulb − annual mean)) + 0.04]; k = 0.03 dry, 0.02 tower, 0.025 adiabatic or hybrid
    2. Grid connection (MVA) = facility peak ÷ 0.95 power factor; voltage ≤ 20 MVA 33 kV, ≤ 80 MVA 66–110 kV, ≤ 300 MVA 220 kV, above 400 kV
    3. Transformers = redundancy(⌈MVA ÷ (3.15 × 0.8)⌉); generators (3 MW) = redundancy(⌈peak ÷ 3⌉); diesel = peak MW × hours × 0.27 kL/MWh
    4. Expected IT peak = Σ zone MW × peak use (training 0.97, inference 0.79, general 0.80)
    5. Liquid heat = Σ zone MW × liquid fraction (direct-to-chip 0.85, immersion 1.0); air heat = IT − liquid + 4% electrical losses; CDUs = redundancy(⌈liquid MW ÷ 1.5⌉)
    6. Heat-rejection units = redundancy(⌈zone peak MW ÷ unit size⌉): dry 1.2, tower 4, adiabatic 1.5, hybrid 2 MW
    7. Network: leaf switches = ⌈GPUs ÷ 32⌉, spines = ⌈leaves × 32 ÷ 64⌉, a core tier above 64 training leaves; front-end = ⌈racks ÷ 2⌉
    8. Peak-day potable draw = on-site kL/day × peak factor (dry 1.3, tower 1.5, adiabatic 3.0, hybrid 2.0) × potable share
    9. Land (m²) = [racks × 4 + heat MW × yard factor (dry 250, tower 60, adiabatic 180, hybrid 150) + 3,000 + 15 × MVA + generators × 60 + battery MWh × 12 + diesel kL × 0.5] × 1.4
    Assumptions
    • Redundancy multiplies counts: N+1 adds one spare per four, 2N doubles, 2N+1 doubles plus one; facility capex × 0.9 (N), 1.0 (N+1), 1.12 (2N), 1.15 (2N+1).
    • Design-day dry-bulb is the ASHRAE 0.4% value from the 14.2 city table; annual means are IMD normals (approximate for Pune, NCR, Kolkata and Visakhapatnam).
    • Equipment sizes, peak factors and land factors are engineering rules of thumb for early design, carried over from the earlier design check.

    Workload split: capacity, energy, water and carbon by zoneEach bar is 100% of the campus. Capacity and energy differ because each zone runs at its own utilisation and PUE.

    How this is calculated · Workload split: capacity, energy, water and carbon by zone
    1. Zone IT energy (MWh) = IT MW × zone share × 8,760 h × zone utilisation
    2. Zone facility energy = zone IT energy × zone PUE; facility PUE = Σ facility ÷ Σ IT (energy-weighted)
    3. Scope 1 water (L) = zone IT kWh × zone WUE; facility WUE = Σ Scope 1 ÷ Σ IT kWh
    4. Scope 2 water (L) = zone facility kWh × [(1 − RE) × grid water + RE × 0.05]
    5. Scope 3 water (L/yr) = GPUs × embodied water per GPU ÷ IT refresh years (AI zones only)
    6. CO₂ (t) = zone facility MWh × (1 − RE) × grid factor
    7. Racks = zone IT kW ÷ rack density, rounded up
    Assumptions
    • PUE and WUE come from the atlas cooling table for the city's climate (plateau, humid coast, hot-dry) unless set by hand.
    • Renewable supply under contract is counted as zero-carbon and 0.05 L/kWh (panel cleaning and manufacturing share).
    • Training and inference both hold GPUs; general-purpose holds CPU servers only.

    Inference capacityHow many queries the inference zone can answer, and what each costs in energy, water, carbon and money.

    How this is calculated · Inference capacity
    1. Inference GPUs = inference IT MW × 1,000 ÷ all-in kW per GPU
    2. Served tokens/s = GPUs × benchmark tokens/s × production efficiency × inference utilisation
    3. Queries per day = served tokens/s ÷ output tokens per query × 86,400
    4. Energy per query (Wh) = inference facility MWh × 10⁶ ÷ queries per year
    5. Water per query (mL) = (inference Scope 1 + Scope 2 litres) × 1,000 ÷ queries per year
    6. Cost per query = inference-zone cost of ownership ÷ queries per year
    Assumptions
    • Benchmark throughput is MLPerf Llama 2 70B in the server scenario (a latency-bounded test); production serving reaches only part of it, hence the efficiency factor.
    • Tokens counted are output tokens; long prompts (prefill) add work not captured here.
    • Google's measured 0.24 Wh per median prompt includes idle capacity, host CPUs and PUE 1.09, so it is a whole-system figure.

    Training capacityHow many training runs of a chosen size the training zone completes in a year, and what each costs.

    How this is calculated · Training capacity
    1. Training GPUs = training IT MW × 1,000 ÷ all-in kW per GPU
    2. Runs per year = training IT MWh per year ÷ IT energy per run
    3. Wall-clock per run (h) = energy per run ÷ (training IT MW × training utilisation)
    4. Carbon per run (t) = energy per run × training PUE × (1 − RE) × grid factor
    5. Water per run (L) = energy per run × 1,000 × [WUE + PUE × ((1 − RE) × grid water + RE × 0.05)]
    Assumptions
    • Energy per run is IT energy: GPU-hours × all-in kW per GPU. Llama 3.1 70B: 7.0 M H100 GPU-hours × 1.4 kW ≈ 9,800 MWh; 405B: 30.84 M × 1.4 ≈ 43,200 MWh; GPT-3: 1,287 MWh.
    • Wall-clock assumes one run at a time using the whole zone.

    Water footprint and what it compares withOn site, at the power stations, and embodied in the GPUs, set against Indian benchmarks.

    How this is calculated · Water footprint and what it compares with
    1. Total water (L/yr) = Scope 1 + Scope 2 + Scope 3
    2. Potable draw = Scope 1 × potable share; treated sewage = the rest
    3. Olympic pools = total ÷ 2.5 million L; people = total ÷ 365 ÷ 135 L/day
    4. Hours of Bengaluru supply = total ÷ 1,935 million L/day × 24
    5. Water per query compared with Google's 0.26 mL per median prompt
    Assumptions
    • Bengaluru supply 1,935 MLD delivered (Karnataka Legislative Council, Aug 2026); urban norm 135 L/person/day (CPHEEO).
    • Scope 3 uses 2.6 m³ per GPU (atlas register, range 1–10) spread over the IT refresh period.

    Grid-interactive economics: battery and training load shiftingWhat a battery earns from demand charges and time-of-day prices, and what moving training out of the evening peak saves.

    How this is calculated · Grid-interactive economics: battery and training load shifting
    1. Battery power (MW) = battery MWh ÷ duration; discharge (MWh/yr) = MWh × depth of discharge × cycles/day × 365
    2. Shaveable peak (MW) = average facility load × peak share; demand cut = min(requested, battery MW, shaveable peak)
    3. Demand-charge saving (₹ cr) = demand cut × 1,000 kVA × ₹/kVA/month × 12 ÷ 10⁷
    4. Time-of-day saving = discharge × 1,000 × spread − discharge × 1,000 × (1/round-trip − 1) × tariff, ÷ 10⁷
    5. Battery net = demand + time-of-day + demand response − O&M (1.5% of capex); payback = battery capex ÷ net
    6. Shifted training energy per day = min(flexible MW × utilisation × peak hours, zone MW × (1 − utilisation) × off-peak hours)
    7. Load-shifting saving = shifted MWh × 1,000 × training PUE × spread ÷ 10⁷
    Assumptions
    • A data centre's load is nearly flat, so a battery can only shave the short peaks above it (cooling peaks on hot afternoons, GPU power swings); the shaveable share is an estimate.
    • Power factor taken as 1 (kVA ≈ kW).
    • India has no demand-response market for data centres yet, so that stream defaults to zero.
    • Training can catch up only in spare capacity, so a zone at 90% utilisation can move little load.
    • For ramp smoothing of training power swings, the earlier design check used 0.5 MWh of battery per MW of IT as a reference (tool heuristic); it is shown in the infrastructure panel, not scored.

    Capital cost and cost of ownershipCapex by item, the yearly operating bill, and capex spread over each asset's life at the cost of capital.

    How this is calculated · Capital cost and cost of ownership
    1. Facility capex = Σ zone MW × base ₹/MW × (cooling system cost ÷ reference system cost) × redundancy factor; plus land, water plant and contingency
    2. IT capex = AI MW × GPU ₹/MW × (1 + duty) + general MW × CPU ₹/MW × (1 + duty)
    3. Battery capex = MWh × 1,000 × $/kWh × ₹/US$ ÷ 10⁷
    4. Capital recovery factor CRF(r, n) = r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)⁻ⁿ); annual capex = facility × CRF(facility life) + IT × CRF(refresh) + battery × CRF(battery life)
    5. Electricity = facility kWh × [(1 − RE) × grid tariff + RE × renewable tariff]; water = potable kL × tariff + treated kL × tariff
    6. Operating cost = electricity + water + O&M + staff + insurance + battery O&M − grid-interactive savings
    7. Cost of ownership = annual capex + operating cost; per GPU-hour = AI-zone cost ÷ (GPUs × 8,760 × utilisation)
    Assumptions
    • Cooling system costs (₹ crore/MW, room system + heat rejection) come from the 14.2 cost tables; the AI base is direct-to-chip with dry coolers (76.5), the general base free-cooling chillers (54.5).
    • All in constant 2026 rupees; no escalation, tax shields or financing fees. Excludes grid interconnection upgrades and working capital.
    • Zone costs allocate facility and IT capex by zone, electricity by zone energy, staff by zone MW.

    Flip the cooling for the AI zonesEvery cooling system that can carry the AI racks, applied to both training and inference zones, with everything else unchanged.

    How this is calculated · Flip the cooling for the AI zones
    1. For each system whose rack ceiling ≥ the larger of the training and inference densities: set both AI zones to it and rerun the whole model
    2. Columns: campus PUE, on-site WUE, total water (Scopes 1–3), CO₂, cost of ownership and the overall verdict
    Assumptions
    • General-purpose zone keeps its own system.
    • Facility capex follows the 14.2 cost ratio for each system, so the cost column includes the cooling plant.

    Sensitivity: what moves the answer mostEach input is moved 20% down and up, one at a time; bars show how far the chosen result moves. The five largest are shown.

    How this is calculated · Sensitivity: what moves the answer most
    1. For each input: result(input × 0.8) and result(input × 1.2), all else unchanged
    2. Swing = |high − low|; inputs ranked by swing
    3. PUE is varied through its overhead (PUE − 1), not PUE itself
    Assumptions
    • A ±20% range is a common, neutral screening range; real uncertainty differs by input (see the ranges in the standards register).
