Cooling systems compared: cost, water and energy

The questionFor each cooling system used in Indian data centres, what does it cost to build and run over its life, how much water does it use, and how much energy?

The short answerFor a conventional hall of about 10 kW per rack, chilled-water air handlers with containment plus cooling towers (best with magnetic-bearing chillers) or indirect evaporative units are cheapest, at about ₹4,000–4,200 per MWh of IT in Bengaluru. Above about 16–30 kW per rack, direct-to-chip liquid cooling becomes cheapest once server-fan savings and floor-space savings are counted, and above about 40–50 kW air is no longer possible. Towers save money but use 35 times the on-site water of dry coolers and more total water everywhere in India. Electricity price, not water price, sets the ranking.

6.2Model · Water

Water: on site, at the power station, and on the peak day

On-site WUE is what the plant evaporates and bleeds off per kWh of IT. Total water adds the water evaporated to make the electricity. Updates with the calculator above.

Water by cooling systemL per kWh of IT on site, and m³ per MWh of IT in total.

Why towers need ~2 L/kWh

  • A tower evaporates about 1.2–1.5 L per kWh of heat, and it must reject IT heat plus chiller work (≈1.1–1.2 kWh per kWh of IT).
  • At 4 cycles of concentration, blowdown is evaporation ÷ 3. Make-up comes to about 1.9–2.3 L/kWh of IT, which confirms the atlas's 1.8 (Bengaluru) to 2.4 (humid coast).
  • Indian municipal water at 400–1,200 mg/L of dissolved solids limits cycles to about 3–5. Going from 3 to 6 cycles cuts make-up by ~20% and blowdown by ~50%.
  • Peak days reach ~2.2× the annual figure, so store 2–5 days of make-up (≈1,000 m³ for a 10 MW tower plant).
  • Reported: a large colocation provider 1.55 L/kWh for evaporative sites; water-cooled chillers in hot regions 2.3–2.8. reported
  • Disagreement: one Indian consultancy guide gives towers 0.6–1.5 L/kWh with a 0.82 worked example, but sizes the tower for 4 MW of heat instead of ~11–12 MW. Its tower figure is understated by about 2.5×; its adiabatic and dry figures are credible.

Power-station water

  • MoEFCC norms: ≤3.5 m³/MWh for existing tower-cooled coal plants (2015); 3.0 for new plants (relaxed from 2.5 in 2018); seawater once-through exempt; amended again July 2025. reported
  • Several plants exceed the norms: one consumed 87% more than the limit (CAG Chennai); Raichur and Ballari in Karnataka also exceed them.
  • With ~70–75% thermal generation, grid consumption at the plant works out to about 2.5–4.2 L/kWh depending on how hydro reservoir evaporation is counted (0 / net / gross gives 2.5 / 4.2 / 9.4 in the Scope 1/2/3 research). The atlas standard is 2.66 L/kWh at the plant for India (73% coal × 3.5 m³/MWh + nuclear and gas), ÷ 0.92 for the ~8% high-voltage network losses a data centre sees = 2.9 L/kWh at the meter; state generation mixes use the same method (Karnataka 1.5, Tamil Nadu 2.1, Andhra Pradesh 2.6, Maharashtra 2.8, Telangana 3.1, Uttar Pradesh 3.3).
  • Result: towers use more total water than air-cooled chillers unless grid water exceeds ~9 L/kWh, and direct-to-chip towers beat dry coolers only above ~35 L/kWh. Indirect evaporative and hybrid coolers are roughly neutral at 2–3 L/kWh.

Water stress and disclosure in India

  • Indian data centres are estimated to use ~150 billion litres in 2025, rising to ~358 billion by 2030 (market research, not metered). estimate S&P Global expects 60–80% of Indian data centres to face high water stress this decade.
  • Bengaluru Rural taluks (Devanahalli, Doddaballapura, Hoskote, Nelamangala) show the district's highest groundwater stress. BWSSB raised non-domestic rates by ₹9–19/kL in April 2025 and added 3% a year escalation.
  • Disclosure is thin and company-wide: CtrlS 409,037 kL (2023-24), saving ~119,662 kL a year by switching to air-cooled systems; Nxtra 306,916 kL (2024-25). reported
  • Karnataka's Sustainable Data Centre Policy 2026–31 (cabinet approval September 2026, ₹1,300.45 cr outlay) requires plant-wise PUE and WUE reporting, PUE-linked incentives and resource heat-mapping for siting.
  • Open towers need a Legionella regime (biocide, risk assessment, cleaning). Adiabatic coolers carry lower risk; dry coolers none.

Chiller efficiency

  • Water-cooled centrifugal: COP 5.4–6.3, or 0.56–0.65 kW/TR (CPWD HVAC spec 2024). reported
  • Magnetic-bearing centrifugal: 0.52 kW/ton at full load, 0.29 at part load (IPLV); studies find 29–67% higher average COP. vendor
  • At 35 °C outdoor: high-lift centrifugal 0.72 kW/ton, dry coolers + magnetic-bearing 0.55, air-cooled scroll 0.89. At 43 °C: 1.06, 0.89 and 1.32, which matters for Delhi NCR and Hyderabad summers.
  • PUE understates liquid cooling: moving 75% of load to direct-to-chip cut facility power by 18.1% and total site power by 10.2%. Server fan power fell 80%, but PUE fell only 3.3%. reported

Which system for which city

Recommendations by cityConventional ≤15 kW per rack; AI ≥40 kW per rack. This is the atlas’s one city-by-city recommendation; Chapter 19 turns it into rules by climate, and Chapter 16 maps every cooling tool.

LocationConventionalAI
Bengaluru, PuneContainment + free-cooling or magnetic-bearing chillers, or a hybrid plant. Towers only on contracted treated sewage.Direct-to-chip W40 + dry coolers with trim (≈65–71% of hours compressor-free). Best location on cost and water.
Hyderabad, Delhi NCRFree-cooling or magnetic-bearing chillers; indirect evaporative in NCR's dry season. Avoid direct evaporative (dust, monsoon humidity).Direct-to-chip + adiabatic coolers; size for 43–45 °C peaks.
Mumbai, ChennaiMagnetic-bearing water-cooled chillers + towers where treated water is secured; otherwise free-cooling air-cooled chillers.Direct-to-chip W40–W45 + towers on treated sewage, or hybrid coolers. Dry-only needs trim chillers much of the year at W32.
Visakhapatnam, JamnagarSeawater with titanium exchangers: cost and permits (CRZ, outfall) unproven; pilot first.Direct-to-chip + seawater exchangers or hybrid coolers; desalinated make-up only with full lifecycle costing.
ImmersionSingle-phase only for niches (edge, retrofits, harsh sites).Do not specify two-phase immersion for new builds.
  • 50 MW AI hall, worked: in Mumbai, towers save about ₹15 crore a year against W40 dry coolers but use ~750 ML a year more water, an implied water price of about ₹200/kL. That makes them rational only on contracted treated sewage. In Bengaluru the dry-cooler premium is about ₹10 crore a year, roughly 0.3% of a ₹3,500–4,750 crore project.
  • Water price rarely decides: towers beat free-cooling chillers until water costs ~₹300–370/kL, and dry coolers in a liquid hall until ~₹240–260/kL, all above Indian tariffs of ₹20–131/kL. The case for dry cooling is availability, permits and risk.
  • Immersion vs direct-to-chip: single-phase immersion wins only above about ₹14–19/kWh, or where the IT maker warrants immersion and all residual air plant is removed.

Evidence is uneven: tariffs, MoEFCC, CEA, Uptime, operator reports and the Vertiv/NVIDIA study are reported; Schneider WP282, immersion PUEs and CDU savings are vendor claims; most per-MW capex and the Indian consultancy figures are estimates. Get one contractor bill of quantities per system before relying on capex better than ±30%.
