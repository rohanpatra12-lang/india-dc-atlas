The questionWhat does a green data centre look like, and what do states require?

The short answerLeaders reach PUE near 1.2 and high clean-power shares. India has about 16 state regimes, no national law in force and almost no water rules.

17.1Report 8 · Green data centres

Green is measured, not labelled

A data centre is green only if it is measured, disclosed and verified on efficiency, hourly carbon-free power, freshwater impact and lifecycle carbon. For a Bengaluru operator the cheapest route is PUE ≤1.20, ≥80% round-the-clock renewables and dry or treated-water cooling. It pays for itself.

17.2Report 9 · State-wise data centre policies

Sixteen states, one race

With no national data-centre law in force, states set the real rules and compete on duty holidays, tariff subsidies and land. Power price, renewables, connectivity and policy stability decide where large projects actually go. Set your own weights and the ranking re-sorts.

Controversy has arrived

  • Visakhapatnam: the Google–Adani campus faces a Jal Biradari PIL on water near Kambalakonda Wildlife Sanctuary, a PIL on 160 acres of temple land, and protests over Dalit farmers' assigned land at Tarluvada.
  • Karnataka's IT minister called AP's Google package (claimed ~₹22,000 crore) "an economic disaster". AP has not published it.
  • CEEW (Feb 2026) found only 5 of 15 state policies had sustainability provisions; about 8 of 16 now do.

Recommendations

  • Centre: a slim national framework of standards: mandatory PUE/WUE/renewable/diesel disclosure for ≥5 MW; climate-zone PUE and WUE benchmarks; a building-code category.
  • Make the 2047 tax holiday conditional on certified reporting, the one lever states cannot undercut.
  • A siting tool combining grid headroom, groundwater stage and climate hazard; a soft cap on stacked subsidies.
  • States: Tamil Nadu and West Bengal should renew now; copy Karnataka's metrics with payouts that matter at hyperscale; publish bespoke deals above 50 MW; spend on substations, treated-water mains and cable landings, not land.
  • Investors: get explicit contract-backed packages in Telangana and AP; price renewal risk in TN and WB; treat Karnataka's reporting as a preview of national rules.
17.3Policy · Approvals

Approvals: what a campus needs, and how long it takes

No Indian law names data centres. A campus is cleared as a building, licensed as a polluter for its generators and cooling, and connected as a large consumer. The grid connection usually sets the date.

Noise: 75 dB(A) at 1 m per set; up to four sets tested together; point-source spreading only (no barriers). Ambient limits: Noise Pollution Rules 2000. reported estimate

Grid reliability: the number nobody publishesGenerators and batteries are sized for outages, but Indian discoms do not report comparable outage statistics.

A 2025 review of outage reporting found that most distribution companies, including those serving Bengaluru, Mumbai and Delhi, publish complaint counts or feeder data rather than SAIDI and SAIFI, and that Delhi's supply code excludes planned outages and interruptions under five minutes. Data centres therefore size diesel for 24–48 hours whatever the grid does. A published, audited reliability index for each data-centre substation would let sites hold less diesel and more batteries, and should be part of any state data-centre policy.
