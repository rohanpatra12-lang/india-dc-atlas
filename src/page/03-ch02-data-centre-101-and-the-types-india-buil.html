Data centre 101 and the types India builds

The questionWhat is a data centre, and what kinds does India build?

The short answerA data centre is a factory that turns electricity into computing and heat. India's 1,789 MW of IT capacity (Cushman & Wakefield, H1 2026; trackers differ by about ±5%) is mostly colocation in Mumbai, while hyperscale cloud and AI campuses now drive the growth.

Scale wins, and India is just getting started

A single facility can be "hyperscale", "AI", "Tier III" and "liquid-cooled" at once, because each label answers a different question. Globally, the economics of scale have pushed capacity from company-owned rooms to cloud giants and colocation. India is small and growing very fast.

Single point of failure. Fifteen of India's 17 international cables land within about 6 km of Versova beach in Mumbai, carrying about 95% of international bandwidth. New east-coast landings (SEA-ME-WE 6 at Chennai, Google's Vizag gateway) matter more than any incentive.
The money already committed. Google ≈US$15 bn for a 1 GW AI hub at Vizag; Microsoft US$17.5 bn; Amazon US$35 bn+ across India by 2030; Reliance up to 3 GW at Jamnagar; Adani ≈US$100 bn by 2035; TCS HyperVault 1 GW+. These are announcements, not finished capacity.

What India should build, in order

  1. Hyperscale and wholesale colocation campuses in five hubs: Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Noida and Vizag. Pair a coastal cable hub with an inland zone 300–700 km away.
  2. Make every large new build AI-ready: 30–130 kW racks, direct-to-chip liquid cooling, PUE ≤1.35. Avoid speculative GPU halls without anchor tenants.
  3. Sovereign and government AI through PPP, extending the IndiaAI "empanel and subsidise" model rather than state-built facilities.
  4. Renewable-backed campuses with round-the-clock contracts; train AI near renewables, serve inference from metros.
  5. A thin layer of modular edge (0.1–2 MW) in Tier-2/3 cities for 5G, UPI and Indian-language inference.

Not priorities: new large enterprise-owned data centres, Tier IV everywhere, underwater designs.
