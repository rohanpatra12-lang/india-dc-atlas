Scope 1, 2 and 3 water: on site, at the power station, and in the supply chain

The questionIf a data centre counted its water the way companies count carbon, how much would each scope be, and which one should India manage first?

The short answerScope 2, the water evaporated at power stations to make the electricity, is 64–96% of the total in every case modelled for India (80–96% with dry or liquid cooling, 64–71% with towers). Scope 1, the site's own cooling, matters only with cooling towers (about 27–33%). Scope 3, the supply chain, is 3–5% even for a GPU-dense hall, and coal mining for the grid is larger than chip-making in it. So dry cooling is the right default, and clean power is the biggest lever: 90% clean power matched hour by hour cuts total water by 60–85%; annual contracts cut it by only 35–50%.

9.1Framework

Three scopes, borrowed from carbon accounting

The GHG Protocol's scopes translate directly to water. Each scope can be reported as withdrawal (water taken), discharge (water returned) and consumption (water evaporated or embodied, the difference).

What each scope includes

ScopeIncludedTypical Indian sources
1 · On siteCooling-tower make-up (evaporation, drift, blowdown), adiabatic pads, indirect evaporative units, humidification, staff and canteen, landscaping, fire-system tests, generator radiatorsBWSSB, MCGM, CMWSSB, HMWSSB supply; borewells under a CGWA permit; tankers; treated sewage
2 · Purchased energyWater used at the power stations that make the electricity: thermal cooling, boiler make-up, ash handling, flue-gas desulphurisation, hydro reservoir evaporation, solar-panel cleaning; also bought chilled waterThe state grid (DISCOM), green open-access contracts, captive renewables, certificates
3 · Value chainFabs for GPUs, memory and storage; servers and boards; copper, steel, concrete; construction water; batteries; diesel; coal mining and washing for the grid's coal; for colocation operators, the tenants' ITTaiwan, Korea, Arizona, Malaysia fabs; Indian cement and steel belts; coalfields in Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand

Convention from Li, Yang, Islam and Ren, "Making AI Less Thirsty" (Communications of the ACM, 2025): on-site cooling is Scope 1, electricity is Scope 2 (location-based), server manufacturing is Scope 3. Embodied water is spread over each asset's life: IT 4–6 years, plant 15, building 25–30. reported

Where the water comes from and where it goes
The same water, in everyday terms
  • Scope 1: IT energy × WUE from the atlas cooling reference (make-up at 4 cycles, rescaled for your cycles). Consumption = make-up × (cycles − 1) ÷ cycles for towers, 90% for adiabatic and evaporative systems, 80% for dry designs. Plus 300 m³ per MW a year for people, landscaping and fire systems, half consumed.
  • Scope 2: facility energy × grid factor. The atlas standard is the India grid value at the meter (2.9 m³/MWh); each state's own generation mix (Karnataka 1.5 … Uttar Pradesh 3.3) is an option. The research factors give consumption with hydro reservoir evaporation excluded, net or gross (India 2.5 / 4.2 / 9.4; Karnataka 1.5 / 4.75 / 14.5) and withdrawal with hydro excluded (India 3.4). Clean power 0.05. Annual contracts physically cover only ~55% of their volume hour by hour; hourly matching covers all of it.
  • Scope 3: GPU about 2.6 m³ each including its share of memory, CPU, rack and inputs (range 1–10); other servers 2.8 m³; building and plant 5,600 m³ per MW over 25 years; coal mining and washing 0.16 m³ per MWh of grid power; diesel 4 m³ per MW a year. Consumption is taken as half of this, because most fab water is recycled or discharged.
  • Stress weights are atlas judgements, ordered by each district's groundwater stage (CGWB) and surface-water stress (on site: Bengaluru 70, Chennai 80, Delhi NCR 90, Hyderabad 60, Mumbai 40, Pune 50; grid 30; chips 15) until basin-level WULCA AWARE 2.0 factors are applied. estimate

Seven metrics every site should publish, monthly

  1. Withdrawal by source (potable, groundwater, tanker, treated, rain)
  2. Consumption (withdrawal − discharge)
  3. Discharge volume and quality
  4. Site WUE, on both withdrawal and consumption basis
  5. Source WUE, including power-station water
  6. Scarcity-weighted footprint (AWARE version declared)
  7. Seasonal profile: peak-day MLD, peak ÷ average, storage hours

Disclosure today: Sify's reported withdrawal fell 20× in a year with no explanation; NTT omitted water in 2025; AdaniConneX has no standalone report.

What to do about each scope

  • Scope 1: closed-loop direct-to-chip with dry coolers for AI halls; free-cooling air-cooled chillers for colocation. Where towers are unavoidable, run them on treated sewage or rainwater at six or more cycles, and recover blowdown with RO. Report consumption and withdrawal separately.
  • Scope 2, the biggest lever: contract wind–solar hybrid or round-the-clock renewables through green open access and move to hourly matching. Each 10 points of clean share cuts about 115,000 kL a year for a 50 MW AI hall on the national grid (78% load, PUE 1.17, 2.9 L/kWh). Prefer sites fed by seawater-cooled or dry-cooled plants.
  • Scope 3: extend hardware life to six years where performance per watt allows; ask chip and server makers for ISO 14046 water data; use blended cement and recycled steel; count upstream coal water, which is larger than fab water.
  • Siting: with stress weighting, coastal Chennai and Mumbai sites on dry cooling and clean power score best; Bengaluru and Delhi NCR sites on towers score worst.

Uncertainties: hydro allocation (0 to over 245 m³/MWh), consumption vs withdrawal in operator reports, embodied chip water (no GPU maker publishes it; 0.1–10 m³ per GPU), the state mixes and the stress weights, which are atlas judgements until basin factors are applied.
