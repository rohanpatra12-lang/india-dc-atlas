Cooling

The questionHow does heat leave the building, and what does each method cost in power and water?

The short answerCooling towers use the least electricity and the most water (about 1.8 L per kWh of IT; 1.5–2.4 by climate). Dry and liquid systems use almost no water but more power in hot hours.

4Report 3 · Every cooling type, what it costs, what India should build

Removing heat is half the design

Cooling has two halves. Inside the room you pull heat away from the chips with air, rear-door coils, cold plates or immersion. Outside you reject it to the atmosphere with towers, adiabatic coolers or dry coolers. The outside choice decides most of the water bill; the inside choice decides how dense the racks can be.

    Assumptions and sources
    • Weather. The atlas's typical hours: monthly normal dry-bulb and wet-bulb with a daily swing (the same hours as the psychrometric chart in 6.3). Real years have hotter extremes.
    • Compressors. Efficiency (COP) = 45% of the ideal (Carnot) value for the evaporating and condensing temperatures, capped at 2–9. Air-cooled condensing at outdoor + 15 °C (DX room units + 18 °C); water-cooled at tower water + 6 °C.
    • Free cooling. Tower water leaves at wet-bulb + 4 K (approach), plus 2 K across the plate heat exchanger. Dry coolers and free-cooling coils leave at dry-bulb + 5–6 K. Free cooling is partial when that water is between supply and return temperature.
    • Water. Tower: 85% of rejected heat leaves by evaporation (1.48 L per kWh), × 4/3 for blowdown at 4 cycles of concentration. Adiabatic pads: 0.148 L per kWh of heat per K of pre-cooling (10 K air rise across the coil), plus 15% bleed; pre-cooling limited to 80% of the gap to wet-bulb. Indirect evaporative: hot-aisle 36 °C, supply 24 °C, heat-exchanger effectiveness 0.7, spray reaching 85% of the wet-bulb gap.
    • Fans and pumps (share of IT): air handlers 4%, pumps 1.5–2%, tower and dry-cooler fans 1–2.5%, DX room units 6% + condenser 2%, CDU and facility pumps 2%. Direct-to-chip: 80% of heat to liquid, 20% via rear doors on 20 °C chilled water. PUE adds 6% for UPS, distribution and lighting.
    • Sources: ASHRAE W17–W45 classes (Upsite); cooling tower approach, evaporation and blowdown (Power Engineering); indirect evaporative modes and 70–80% wet-bulb effectiveness (CSE); adiabatic coolers (DCD); CDU capacities and loop temperatures (Motivair); single- and two-phase immersion (NADDOD).
    • This is a teaching model. The reference PUE and WUE values used elsewhere in the atlas come from the standards register and the cooling table in chapter 6.

    Why liquid winsHeat a cubic metre of fluid carries per degree (kJ/m³·K), log scale.

    Moving 100 kW with a 10 K rise needs about 8 m³/s of air but only 2.4 L/s of water. That is why fan power and air-handler size collapse once heat is caught at the chip.

    How the hyperscalers cool

    • Google: liquid-cooled TPUs since 2018; says its Vizag AI hub will use air-cooled (dry) heat rejection.
    • Microsoft: since August 2024 every new design is closed-loop "zero water evaporation", avoiding 125 million L a year per site. Fleet WUE 0.30 L/kWh.
    • Meta: air-assisted liquid cooling (cold plates dumping heat into room air via rear-door coils) in existing halls.
    • AWS: In-Row Heat Exchanger (July 2025) to run GB200 racks in air-cooled halls.

    Indian deployments

    • NTT NAV1, Mahape: 4.8 MW immersion + direct-contact cooling (2023), ~30% more efficient.
    • Sify: 130 kW/rack Rabale; 200 kW/rack Chennai and Noida (DGX-Ready).
    • Yotta: RDHx to 50–60 kW, DLC to 80 kW per rack.
    • Local supply: Schneider/Motivair liquid-cooling factory in Bengaluru (2026); Vertiv in Pune (200 W–2 MW+).
    • Leading operators report PUE 1.4–1.5; MeitY AI tenders want <1.35.
