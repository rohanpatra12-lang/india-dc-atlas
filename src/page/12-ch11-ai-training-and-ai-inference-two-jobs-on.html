AI training and AI inference: two jobs, one kind of building

The questionHow does a data centre that trains AI models differ from one that answers people's prompts, and how much electricity and water does one prompt really cost?

The short answerThe chips and racks are now largely the same. What differs is how the load behaves and where it sits: training runs thousands of GPUs flat out, in lockstep, for weeks, wherever power is cheap and plentiful; inference follows the day's demand from users, sits near cities and carries more backup. One short prompt uses a quarter to a third of a watt-hour (0.24–0.34 Wh, measured by Google and OpenAI) and about 0.6–1.8 mL of water once the power station is counted, but billions a day add up: over a model's life, serving it uses more energy and water than training it.

11.1Research · Hardware

Two jobs on increasingly similar machines

Training builds a model; inference uses it. A useful picture: training is writing and printing a textbook once, and inference is every reader opening it, every day, for years.

Training: teaching the model

The model reads trillions of words (tokens), guesses the next one, checks the answer and adjusts billions of internal numbers. It does this millions of times. The work is split across thousands of GPUs that must finish each step together before any can start the next, so they all run, and pause, at once.

  • One job on 16,000 to over 200,000 GPUs
  • Weeks to months of non-stop running (Llama 3.1 405B: 16,384 H100s for 54 days)
  • Can wait or move: nobody is waiting for an answer, so it can go where power is cheap
  • Restarts from a saved copy (a checkpoint) if a GPU fails, which happened every ~3 hours on Llama 3.1

Inference: answering prompts

A finished model receives a prompt, reads it (the "prefill" step) and writes the reply one token at a time (the "decode" step). Each request is small and runs on one to a few racks; thousands of copies of the model serve millions of people at once.

  • Millions of small jobs, each lasting seconds
  • Follows people's day: busier in the evening, quieter at night
  • Must be fast and always on: sits near users, with full backup power
  • Lasts as long as the model is in use, often years, so its total grows past training

Lower precision

Training mostly works with 16- or 8-bit numbers. Inference is moving to 8- and 4-bit numbers, and each halving roughly doubles the answers per watt. That is a big reason Google's energy per prompt fell 33× in one year.

Memory is the bottleneck

Writing a reply token by token is limited by how fast the chip can read the model from memory, not by raw compute. That is why inference chips advertise memory size and speed, and why NVIDIA splits prefill (CPX) from decode (HBM GPUs).

Batches

Training feeds huge batches through thousands of GPUs at once. Inference batches are limited by how long a user will wait for the first word, so its GPUs average well below peak power.

11.2Research · Power

How the two loads behave on the grid

Training is a flat plateau with sharp, synchronised swings every second or two. Inference rises and falls with people's day. That difference, not rack density, is what separates the two for power planners.

Why grid operators worry about trainingRules are arriving in the US; India has none yet.

  • Big, fast swings. A training campus can drop around 300 MW "in moments" and ramp back. NERC also recorded an unexpected loss of about 1,500 MW of data-centre load that the grid had to absorb.
  • Oscillations. Swings at 0.2–3 Hz can excite resonances in generators and lines, the same problem steel arc furnaces cause.
  • New rules. NERC issued a Level 3 Essential Action Alert on computational loads on 4 May 2026 (responses due 3 August 2026). Texas (ERCOT NOGRR282) took effect on 1 August 2026.
  • Fixes. Filler work that keeps GPUs busy during pauses (under 5% throughput loss, but wasted energy), GPU firmware that limits ramps, and batteries at the rack or the site.
  • The upside. Training can pause or move, so it can act as demand response. xAI got grid power within a year by agreeing to be interruptible. Inference can only shift between regions.

NERC Large Loads Task Force; NERC Level 3 alert (May 2026); ERCOT. reported

Is India training or serving?

By hardware and by number of users, India is building for inference. By GPU-hours actually used today, sovereign model training may still dominate.

Where each kind belongs in IndiaSiting follows from the load behaviour in 11.2.

  • Inference near users and cables: Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Delhi NCR, and Visakhapatnam as a new cable hub. Full backup power; a share of air-cooled servers that will lean on evaporative cooling and city water.
  • Training where power is cheap and clean: Gujarat (Jamnagar), Rajasthan, coastal Andhra Pradesh, with captive solar, wind and storage. Water and carbon should use the local or captive power mix, not the national average.
  • Training can run dry. Even at Indian temperatures, 40–45 °C liquid loops can reject heat through dry coolers for most of the year, so a training hall need not evaporate water at all.
  • Plan for the cascade. Halls built for training in 2026 will likely serve inference within 2–4 years as GPUs age, so build them with the backup and water plans inference needs.
