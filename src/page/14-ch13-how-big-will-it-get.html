How big will it get?

The questionHow much capacity and electricity will India's data centres need?

The short answerMarket trackers give 4.2–8 GW of IT and 31–60 TWh a year by 2030 (Wood Mackenzie: 12 GW); NITI Aayog's scenario implies about 90 TWh; the government's grid queue implies 140–175 TWh by 2031-32. The units differ, so compare carefully.

Most of the spread is units, not optimism: grid load is IT capacity × PUE plus headroom, and connection requests are not consumption. NITI Aayog's 16 GW comes with a 40% utilisation, so its electricity (~90 TWh) sits between the market and the government cases. reported

How this is calculated · forecast to 2035
  1. CAGR: C(y) = 1,611 × (1 + g)^(y − 2025)
  2. Logistic: C(y) = K ÷ (1 + e^(−r(y − t₀))), with t₀ = 2025 + ln(K ÷ 1,611 − 1) ÷ r so the curve passes through end-2025
  3. Pipeline: C(y) = 1,789 + 509 × c_UC × f_UC(y) + 3,351 × c_P × f_P(y) + X × f_P(y); f_UC = 0.4, 0.9, 1 for 2026, 2027, 2028+; f_P = 0, 0.12, 0.37, 0.65, 1 for 2026–2030
  4. Integrated: 2026–2030 = mean(pipeline, logistic); 2031–2035 = logistic × delivery factor (base 0.9)
  5. Electricity: IT TWh = MW × u × 8.76 ÷ 1,000; facility TWh = IT × PUE; u and PUE move linearly 2026 → 2035
  6. India requirement (BU) = 1,694 × (1 + g_grid)^(y − 2024); share = facility TWh ÷ BU
  7. Carbon: net Mt = facility TWh × EF(y) × (1 − clean(y)); location-based Mt = facility TWh × EF(y). EF and clean share are piecewise linear through 2026, 2030 and 2035
  8. Liquid / dry share (Bass): L = 0.20 + (m − 0.20) × F(y − 2025), F(t) = (1 − e^(−(p+q)t)) ÷ (1 + (q/p)e^(−(p+q)t))
  9. Conventional WUE = L × 0.1 + (1 − L) × W_air, with W_air = e × WUE_evap + (1 − e) × 0.5 and evaporative share e falling 0.69 → 0.50
  10. AI share of capacity A: 15% (2026) → your 2030 and 2035 values; AI halls run harder, so their share of IT energy is A × 1.2 ÷ (A × 1.2 + 1 − A)
  11. AI rack density k moves linearly 2026 → 2035; share of heat to liquid f = 0 at 20 kW, 0.9 × (k − 20) ÷ 110 up to 130 kW, then up to 1.0 at 250 kW
  12. AI WUE = f × (dry share × 0.05 + (1 − dry share) × 1.5) + (1 − f) × W_air
  13. Fleet WUE = AI energy share × AI WUE + (1 − AI energy share) × conventional WUE; on-site MLD = IT TWh × WUE × 2.7397
  14. Power-plant water: L per kWh bought = clean share × source water (solar/wind 0.05, RTC 0.07, reservoir hydro 10, nuclear 2.6, mix 1.93) + (1 − clean) × grid water; grid water = 2.9 L/kWh × EF(y) ÷ 0.675, so it falls as coal's share falls; MLD = facility TWh × L/kWh × 2.7397
  15. Build cost (air) = 58.4 × (C(y) ÷ 1,611)^(−b) × (1 + π)^(y − 2025); AI = air × (1 + a); blended = air × (1 + a × AI share), AI share 0.4 → 0.6 (2030) → 0.7 (2035)
  16. Capex (₹ crore) = Σ ΔMW × blended cost; excludes land and IT/GPU hardware
  17. GPU price (₹/hr) = P₀ × (1 − d)^(y − 2026)
  18. Cities 2026–2030: increment over 1,789 MW split 8.6% Vizag, 5.2% other, rest by C&W pipeline shares; 2031–2035 each year's increment split α × current share + (1 − α) × score^k ÷ Σ score^k
  19. Monte Carlo: 1,000 runs; K lognormal (σ 0.36) around your value, other inputs triangular or uniform around your values (ranges in the parameter table); K and utilisation correlated (ρ ≈ 0.5). Band = 10th–90th percentile

How the best and worst cases are built
  • Best case: for each hall type, the allowed cooling with the least total water per kWh (on site plus the power-station water its PUE causes, at the power supply you chose); the city's annual climate (days-weighted average of 12 monthly values); towers at 6 cycles of concentration (softened or treated make-up); load 10 points below the norm if load uncertainty is on.
  • Worst case: the allowed cooling with the most total water per kWh; the city's hottest month for that design; towers at 3 cycles (untreated make-up); load 10 points above the norm. It is a peak-month daily rate, not an annual average.
  • Halls: colocation (load 70%, air-cooled, ~8–15 kW racks), hyperscale (75%, air-cooled), AI (80%, with the rack density you choose; 40 kW racks are air-cooled, NVL72 sends 85% of heat to liquid, Vera Rubin 90%, Kyber 100%). Liquid-cooled heat can go to any of the four systems; "liquid + dry coolers" cannot serve air-cooled halls, which then use air-cooled chillers.
  • Capacity: live and pipeline MW by city from Cushman & Wakefield H1 2026; Visakhapatnam's ~1 GW (Google–Adani) is announced, not in that pipeline. Supplies: BMC ~4,100, HMWSSB ~2,478, Metrowater 1,245, DJB ~4,555, PMC ~1,222, BWSSB ~1,935, KMC ~1,850, GVMC ~384 MLD. Kolkata has no climate table in the atlas and uses Chennai's (both hot and humid).
  • Power-station water uses the India grid factor (2.9 L/kWh at the meter for every city; state generation mixes 1.5–3.5 as a sensitivity) for the grid share and 0.05 L/kWh for clean power.

What the forecast means

  • Planners and utilities: plan transmission for the high case (about 14 GW connected load by 2032, close to the CEA's 17 GW and MoP's 13.56 GW estimates). Over-building wires is cheap; stranding a 1 GW campus is not.
  • Investors: underwrite at no more than 0.7× of announced pipeline, and model ₹ crore per MW rising 2–4% a year, not falling.
  • States: make dry or recycled-water cooling a condition of approval in Chennai, Navi Mumbai and Visakhapatnam, and tie incentives to measured, published PUE and WUE.
  • Biggest swing factors: power and grid connection (±5 GW by 2035), the AI demand cycle (±3–6 GW), GPU export controls, policy, water. Rebase every six months to C&W or JLL actuals.
