Energy and carbon

The questionHow much electricity do data centres use, and how clean is it?

The short answerEvery kWh from India's grid emits about 0.675 kg of CO₂ (CEA, FY2025-26; 0.727 two years earlier), and about 0.96 kg at the margin, where added load is met by coal. An AI campus runs fuller and at a lower PUE than a colocation hall, and when and where it draws power matters as much as how much.

7.1Report 5 · Every TWh is not the same

Same electricity, very different footprint

The carbon and water behind a kilowatt-hour depend on five things: source, timing, location, carbon intensity and water intensity. One TWh can mean about 10,000 tCO₂ or about 1,000,000 tCO₂. Where, when and from what you buy power matters more than almost any efficiency gain inside the building.

Real Indian coal units run at 900–1,100 g. CEA grid average 675 g (FY2025-26); Ember 670 g (2025); Karnataka's consumption mix ≈500 g (estimate). The NEP pathway implies ~430 g by FY2031-32.

Water consumed by sourceL/MWh, typical. Indian coal plants use about twice the world average.

CSE found about half of non-seawater coal capacity broke the 3.5 m³/MWh limit. Raichur ran 87% over it and has shut for lack of Krishna water.

Location-based rose 130% from FY20 to FY24 while market-based fell 43%. Google reached 66% hourly carbon-free energy globally in 2024 but only 12% in Asia-Pacific. The GHG Protocol is moving to hourly matching (~2027).

What to do

  • Operators: contract 24/7, not annual: solar + wind hybrids with 2–4 h batteries via green open access or group captive, targeting ≥80–90% hourly CFE.
  • Shift flexible jobs into the eight solar hours; sign demand-response deals with BESCOM/KPTCL like Google's with TVA.
  • Report location-based and market-based Scope 2, hourly CFE%, and site + source water.
  • Regulators: CEA should publish hourly and marginal emission factors and a residual mix.
  • Publish metered plant-level water use; enforce the 3.5/3.0 m³/MWh norms.
  • Karnataka should adopt Andhra Pradesh's announced 70% renewable rule, but measured hour by hour.
