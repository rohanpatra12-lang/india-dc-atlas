AI or conventional: what India is building, and what it will drink

The questionHow different is an AI data centre from a conventional one, how much of India's capacity is AI today, and what happens to water if the coming capacity is mostly AI?

The short answerPer megawatt, the two use similar water for the same cooling; AI packs 10–50× more power into each rack and builds 5–20× bigger sites. India today is overwhelmingly conventional (AI hardware is roughly 2–3% of live IT load), but forecasters expect AI to be over half of capacity by 2030. If the atlas's central ~3 GW of new capacity, three-quarters AI, is cooled with towers on grid power, India's data centres would use as much water as about six integrated steel plants (KPMG's 5.5 GW: about nine and a half); with liquid cooling to dry coolers and 90% clean power, under one.

10.4Report 13 · Conventional vs AI data centres

Same efficiency, five times the footprint

An AI campus is not a wasteful data centre. Per megawatt of IT it uses about the same energy as a good colocation hall, and often less water on site. What changes is scale and density: racks ten times hotter, campuses five to fifty times bigger. In India, most of the water it uses is evaporated at coal plants, so the power contract matters more than the cooling plant.

The gap opens per rack and per site, because AI packs more power into each rack, runs it almost flat out, and is built at far larger scale. Move the sliders and every number below is recalculated.

How this is calculated · why AI data centres use more water
  1. Water per kWh of heat, fully evaporated = 3.6 MJ ÷ 2.43 MJ/kg (latent heat at ~30 °C) = 1.48 L
  2. Cooling tower: ~80% of heat leaves by evaporation (1.19 L/kWh); blowdown = evaporation ÷ (cycles of concentration − 1) = 0.40 L at 4 cycles; total 1.58 L per kWh of heat rejected
  3. Air-cooled hall on chillers: the tower also rejects the chiller's compressor heat, × (1 + 1/COP 6) = 1.84 → WUE 1.8 L/kWh of IT. Warm-water direct-to-chip runs without chillers most of the year, so the tower rejects about the IT heat alone → WUE 1.5
  4. IT load factor = idle share × (1 − compute utilisation) + busy power share × compute utilisation (LBNL 2024 server model)
  5. Per MW per year: IT energy = load factor × 8,760 MWh; on-site water = IT energy × WUE ÷ 1,000 (ML); power-station water = IT energy × PUE × grid water ÷ 1,000 (ML)
  6. Per rack per day: rack kW × load factor × 24 h × (WUE + PUE × grid water) ÷ 1,000 for kL; on-site only uses WUE alone
  7. Per site: per-MW totals × site MW
  8. Ratio at each level = AI value ÷ conventional value
Assumptions
  • Conventional: idle 36% of maximum power, 100% when busy (LBNL 2024, 2023 values); default compute utilisation 30% (LBNL colocation 21–35%); ~10 kW per rack (Uptime 2024 average ~8 kW).
  • AI: idle 20% of rated power, 78% of rated when training (LBNL 2024; measured 8-GPU H100 node 1.86 kW idle, ~8.4 kW peak, arXiv 2412.08602); default utilisation 80%; 135 kW per rack (GB300 NVL72 132–142 kW, Lenovo/HPE specifications).
  • PUE and WUE from the atlas cooling reference (Chapter 6, Bengaluru; the simulator and Chapter 16 use the same values): towers — conventional 1.25 / 1.8, AI 1.10 / 1.8; dry — conventional air-cooled chillers 1.45 / 0.05, AI liquid + dry coolers 1.15 / 0.05.
  • Grid water at the meter: India grid 2.9 L/kWh at the meter (CEA publishes one national grid; carbon uses the same boundary); marginal 3.8.
  • Chip manufacturing water is not included; no site discloses it.

Five reasons the AI site uses more waterNone of them is that a GPU's heat is harder to remove than a CPU's.

  1. Density. One GB300 rack carries the heat of about fourteen conventional racks, so one hall, one town supply and one substation carry what used to be spread over many sites.
  2. It runs flat out. Training keeps GPUs busy around 80% of the time at close to full power, so water demand does not dip at night or at weekends. Peak-day demand stays near the average, with the summer evaporation peak on top.
  3. Scale. AI campuses are 100 MW to 1 GW where a colocation hall is 5–20 MW. The IEA expects AI to drive about half of the growth in world data-centre electricity to 2030.
  4. The electricity behind it. In India about 70% of the total is evaporated at power stations, roughly 3 L for every kWh bought. More electricity means more water, wherever the site is.
  5. Towers are the cheapest way to dump heat at scale. Many AI builds choose evaporative towers for cost, even though warm-water liquid cooling could run on dry coolers.

And what it does not meanThree common misreadings of "AI is thirsty".

  • Not more water per kWh. Evaporating water absorbs 2.43 MJ per kg whatever produced the heat. AI halls on liquid cooling often use slightly less per kWh, because warm water needs no chiller.
  • Not more water per MW. AI's higher load factor (about 66% against 55%) is mostly offset by its lower PUE (about 1.10 against 1.25 on towers), so a megawatt of AI capacity uses only about 10% more water a year than a megawatt of colocation.
  • Not fixed. On dry coolers, a 100 MW AI campus cuts on-site water by about 98%, but total water only by about 28%, because the power-station water barely moves. Clean, hour-matched power is the bigger lever (Chapters 16 and 18).

Does liquid cooling save water? Run the tradeLiquid cooling lets heat leave through dry coolers instead of evaporating it. That costs extra electricity, and in India extra electricity carries coal-plant water and CO₂. Move the sliders to see who wins.

How this is calculated · going dry
  1. IT energy (GWh) = MW × load factor × 8.76
  2. On-site water saved (ML) = IT GWh × (WUE wet − WUE dry)
  3. Extra electricity (GWh) = IT GWh × PUE penalty
  4. Extra grid water (ML) = extra GWh × (1 − clean share) × grid L/kWh · extra CO₂ (kt) = extra GWh × 0.675 × (1 − clean share)
  5. Net water = saved − extra grid water
  6. Break-even grid intensity (L/kWh) = (WUE wet − WUE dry) ÷ PUE penalty; going dry saves water whenever the grid is below this

Policy hooksThe two rules that touch AI efficiency and water.

  • IndiaAI GPU tender (MeitY corrigendum, Sept 2024): bidders affirm PUE "not greater than 1.35". Industry said legacy sites could not meet it. There is no WUE counterpart.
  • Karnataka Sustainable DC Policy 2026–31: ₹1,300.45 crore outlay, 1 GW by 2031, incentives tied to power and water efficiency, PUE and WUE monitoring, treated water, zones outside Bengaluru. Before it, neither BWSSB nor KSPCB tracked data-centre water.

What the comparison says to do

  • Report three waters, never WUE alone: on-site, grid (2.9 L/kWh at the meter for India, from MoEFCC limits and measured plant averages; range 2.3–3.5; 3.8 at the margin; Karnataka) and the total.
  • Compare per MW of IT, with the load factor stated. Per-site comparisons mostly measure size.
  • Buy clean power first. In India, moving from coal to solar and wind saves more water than any cooling technology.
  • Coastal AI sites: dry or seawater by default. Flag any Bengaluru, Hyderabad or NCR proposal that relies on evaporative cooling or groundwater.
  • Add WUE to the IndiaAI PUE < 1.35 rule, and ask Karnataka to publish facility-level PUE and WUE.
  • Disclose the AI campuses. Not one of the world's flagship AI sites has published an operating PUE or WUE.
